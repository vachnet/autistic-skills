# Sources and evidence boundaries

Official sources checked 2026-10-24. Platform rules and interfaces may change; recheck before public submission.

1. [OpenAI: Is ChatGPT safe for all ages?](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313401-is-chatgpt-safe-for-all-ages). Under-13 educational interaction must be adult-conducted; ages 13–18 require parental consent. This constrains who may use these skills directly.
2. [Claude: Get started](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114491-get-started-with-claude). Claude consumer services require users to be at least 18. The minor pathway here is adult preparation of offline materials, not shared-account child use.
3. [Claude: Use skills](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12512180-use-skills-in-claude). Documents individual ZIP upload, required code-execution capability, and organizational sharing. Does not establish a public consumer skills marketplace for this project.
4. [OpenAI: Build skills](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/build-skills). Skill folder structure, discovery, and plugin-based distribution. Feature availability differs by surface/account.
5. [OpenAI: Package a plugin](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/build/plugins). Portable root manifest, root skills directory, OpenAI UI metadata, local marketplace, and relative asset paths.
6. [OpenAI: Upload and submit](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/deploy/submission). Submission ZIP, verified developer identity, publisher permissions, scanning/review, and publication. Uploading a ZIP locally is not public publication.
7. [ASHA: Augmentative and Alternative Communication](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/professional-issues/augmentative-and-alternative-communication/). Describes varied communication methods, individualized assessment, core/fringe vocabulary, communicative functions including refusal/questions, and approaches involving consistent motor plans. This informed design choices; it is not validation of these skills or proof that chat can replace AAC.

## Original case supplied by the project owner

The Thai text describes stakeholder interviews at an autism association, caregiver questionnaires, a communication app with subject/action/object/modifier choices, confirmation, photo/audio vocabulary, and a phased Expo implementation. No article URL, author identity, screenshots, source code, permission to reuse images, raw questionnaire responses, or clinical outcomes were supplied.

We extracted product ideas and wrote new instructions and fictional examples. No article passages, association logos, child drawings/photos, third-party vocabulary datasets, or code were redistributed. No institutional endorsement is claimed.

## Product decisions and unknowns

One-question turns, up to three options, Thai default, optional English/bilingual display, a three-exchange practice default, stable text menus, and short-model scaffolding are prototype design choices. They need participatory testing with autistic communicators. They are not clinical recommendations or validated treatment parameters.

The supplied account's generalizations that autistic people cannot describe their needs, cannot type, or all need the same interface were not adopted. Caregiver accounts are useful but do not replace the communicator's preferences. Internet word-frequency lists are not an individualized communication assessment.
